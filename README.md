## Overview

This manuscript explores the hypothesis that continuous spacetime geometry may be a useful macroscopic description rather than a fundamental feature of the universe. It proposes a discrete causal-counting picture and an information-resistance matrix as an alternative conceptual framework for describing gravitational phenomena. It also suggests investigating whether certain high-dimensional algebraic structures, including Shimura varieties and their orbits, could provide a mathematical language for transitions in that framework.

The paper is intended to present a research hypothesis and invite critical examination. Its claims should not be read as established physics or as experimentally verified results.

## Abstract

The manuscript questions whether continuous geometric spacetime must be treated as ontologically fundamental. It proposes replacing a continuous microscopic time background with discrete causal iterations related to the Planck length and the speed of light, and reframes gravity through an information-resistance matrix governed by an action principle. It further proposes exploring gravitational escape as a possible form of non-continuous modular-orbit transition in a high-dimensional algebraic setting.

These are conjectural proposals. Establishing their physical validity would require rigorous mathematical formulation, recovery of established results, and testable predictions.

## Research Questions

- Can a discrete causal model describe microscopic dynamics without assuming an external continuous time parameter?
- Under what conditions could macroscopic time and familiar spacetime geometry emerge from such a model?
- Can the proposed information-resistance formulation be made mathematically consistent with established gravitational physics?
- Is there a precise, physically meaningful mapping between the proposed transitions and Shimura-variety orbit structures?
